# TEFCA in 2026: What to Know Before Your Payer Asks About It

Something we have been hearing more often in the last six months: health system IT directors who thought TEFCA was a future problem are getting asked about it by payers and contracting teams right now.

This is not a compliance post written by a policy expert. It is a ground-level read from someone who has been inside a lot of healthcare IT environments and keeps running into the same gaps. Take it as orientation, not legal guidance.

---

## What TEFCA Actually Is

TEFCA stands for Trusted Exchange Framework and Common Agreement. At the practical level, it establishes a set of rules for how healthcare organizations share patient data with each other across different systems and networks.

The mechanism is a network of QHINs — Qualified Health Information Networks. Think of them as the roads that connect different organizations' systems. To participate, your systems need to be able to send and receive data in a way that meets the technical requirements those networks define.

The short version: if your patient portal, EHR, or data systems were built more than a few years ago, they may not be able to communicate in the format that TEFCA exchange requires.

---

## Why It Is Coming Up Now

A few things have pushed this from future requirement to present conversation:

CMS tied TEFCA participation to certain value-based care programs. Payers working under those programs are now asking about readiness when contracts come up for renewal.

Prior authorization rules finalized in 2024 require payers to support specific data exchange formats starting in 2027. Organizations that contract with those payers need compatible systems on their end to participate.

The QHIN network is live. Organizations that expected to have more runway are discovering that the organizations they need to connect with are already on it.

---

## The Most Common Gap We See

Patient portals and clinical systems built before 2021 or so were often designed around older data exchange formats. They can move records from one place to another, but not necessarily in the specific way TEFCA exchange requires.

When we walk through a new environment, we are usually looking at whether there is a translation layer between the organization's existing systems and the format required for exchange. Sometimes one already exists and just needs to be connected. Sometimes it does not exist and needs to be built or purchased.

The organizations that are in the best position did a baseline assessment of where their systems stood at least a year before they needed to act on it. The ones in the hardest spot are trying to figure it out under a deadline.
